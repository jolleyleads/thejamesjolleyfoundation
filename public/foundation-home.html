The young man behind the mission

James Michael Jolley

September 2, 2008 — October 11, 2025

James was a son, a friend, and a young man whose life mattered far beyond the circumstances of his death. He loved sports, his people, and the future that should have been ahead of him.

After James died at age 17, his father turned heartbreak into a commitment: to help other parents find support and to push for a system that responds to teenagers in crisis with urgency and compassion.

We remember James not only for how he died, but for the love, joy, and possibility he brought into the world.

“No family should have to wonder whether help could have arrived sooner.”

James’s Obituary

James Michael Jolley · Forever 17

James Michael Jolley was born in Portsmouth, Virginia, on September 2, 2008. He passed away on October 11, 2025, at just 17 years old. His loss is deeply felt by the many relatives and friends who loved him.

James attended Christopher Academy, Randolph-Macon Academy, and Western Branch High School. A talented and competitive athlete, he enjoyed baseball, basketball, and soccer. He also loved time near the water. From his first beach trip as a baby, he grew to enjoy boogie boarding, skimboarding, and surfing. Fishing with friends and playing video games were among the other things that brought him joy.

He is lovingly remembered by his father, Matthew Ryan Jolley; grandparents James Kenneth “Kenny” Jolley, Amy Luallin, and Angela Godfrey; and his special grandmother, Paula Arcese. His family also includes uncle Christopher B. Jolley; aunts Jessica Tennant and Jamie Lee Godfrey; cousins Jax Jolley, Parker Tennant, and Levi Tennant; and great-grandmother Mary Ann Elgin. James was preceded in death by his great-grandfather, Kenneth B. Jolley.

More than any list of dates or achievements, James leaves memories of a son, grandson, nephew, cousin, and friend who was loved beyond words. His family carries that love forward, and the Foundation honors his life by working to help other young people and families.

This is a family tribute adapted from the published obituary. For the original wording, condolences, and funeral-home information, visit the official notice.

What we stand for

Turning loss into action

When a teenager needs help, a complicated system should never be another barrier. Our mission centers on treatment access, family guidance, and education.

ACCESS

Faster paths to treatment

Advocating for more timely, practical ways for young people and families to connect with appropriate substance-use care.

SUPPORT

Families deserve answers

Helping families understand their next steps and find resources during some of the hardest moments of their lives.

AWARENESS

Prevention through education

Starting honest conversations about fentanyl, overdose prevention, adolescent substance use, and the urgency of early intervention.

For families

Need help finding a way forward?

If your teenager is struggling with substance use or you are trying to navigate treatment options, reach out to tell us what you need. We can discuss resources and possible next steps; contacting us does not guarantee placement, funding, or immediate response.

Reach out

Send a brief message describing the situation. Please avoid emailing private medical records or sensitive personal details.

Explore options

Discuss treatment-access barriers and resources that may be available for your family.

Keep advocating

Use trusted medical and community support while you work toward an appropriate care plan.

Email a Support Request

If someone is overdosing or in immediate danger, call 911. In the United States, call or text 988 for crisis support. This website is not an emergency response service.
